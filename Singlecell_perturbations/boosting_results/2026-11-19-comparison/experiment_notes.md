## Design and selection

The observation is a cell type and drug pair. All four validation folds use the saved sparse-cell splits (NK, CD4, CD8, Treg). A gene-space RMSE is calculated for each held-out pair by averaging squared error over its 18,211 genes and taking the square root. The fold score averages those row errors. Selection gives each completed fold one quarter of the weight. No leaderboard score enters selection.

A uses cell-type and drug identity indicators and directly predicts 18,211 genes. B uses the same 152 input features and predicts 160 principal components. C adds 22,787 baseline RNA features to A. D adds the same features to B. The six donor-balanced cell-type profiles are looked up from the precomputed baseline_expression_donor_balanced_v2.csv. The baseline RNA measurements were averaged within donor and cell type with implicit zeros included; each donor then received equal weight. No new RNA embedding or RNA feature PCA was introduced. The encoder and randomized target PCA use training rows only (seed 42). PCA predictions are inverse-transformed before scoring. RNA supplies a fixed profile per cell type; it does not give each validation drug a new measured treatment response.

## Decision timeline

1. Reused XGBoost T00 and T09 from the previous Expanse archive. Reused PyBoost A, B and D after rescoring all twelve saved validation predictions against the original labels. Colab timing is reported separately from current Expanse timing. The current job measured new configurations only.
2. Measured all four XGBoost D baseline folds. XGBoost A completed its NK fold in about 868 seconds. Its remaining folds were deferred until the PCA configurations and tree-strategy comparison had run. XGBoost C reached its 900-second NK trial limit. PyBoost C failed on the 32 GiB V100 while attempting to allocate a further 21,054,101,504 bytes. Neither C configuration supplied a four-fold score.
3. Tested B and D for each library. XGBoost B already had a full historical search. For XGBoost D, the first candidate changed depth and L2 together. The next held those fixed and jointly changed learning rate and rounds. For PyBoost, increasing depth alone from 3 to 4 helped D; the slower learning rate with additional rounds helped D again. Both PyBoost changes worsened B. Baseline PyBoost A and B differed by approximately 0.00043 MRRMSE. The historical four-fold time was about 2,030 seconds for A and 43 seconds for B, supporting the choice of B alongside the stronger D. XGBoost A was still incomplete when the tuning shortlist was chosen.
4. Switched the XGBoost T09 tree strategy with its remaining settings fixed. Shared multi-output trees were faster in these runs, with worse mean validation MRRMSE. T09 favored one tree per output. PyBoost used its own shared-tree implementation (SingleSplitter). Averaged timings from different libraries or hardware should be interpreted with their recorded scopes.
5. Job 55870938 saved the validation and strategy results, then failed during the pre-final time check because squeue was unavailable on the compute-node PATH. Job 55873673 used the remaining time as reported by the scheduler or allocated time limit, reused the NK result, and resumed the other XGBoost A folds. The definitive selections are recorded in the final_selection journal event after checking A and before either full-data fit.

## Compute and evidence

One Tesla V100-SXM2-32GB was allocated at a time. Slurm elapsed time summed over both allocations is the GPU-hour accounting basis. Per-fit model time, preprocessing time and the sampled peak memory of the training process are separate measurements. Historical results carry their original timing scopes. Per-process memory sampling can miss short peaks. Runs used a fixed number of rounds, float32 model inputs and the gene-space metric.
